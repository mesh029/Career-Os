### Assessment — four questions (paste)

**Explain a complex issue to an executive or non-technical user**

A program lead thought SharePoint was "broken" because a folder had vanished. It had not. Permissions had changed after an offboarding. I showed her the site in the browser, who still had access, and what I was about to restore. No jargon. She saw the file, I wrote a five-line how-to, and she stopped calling it an outage.

**SharePoint Online and OneDrive permissions**

I start at the site, not the folder. Check the Microsoft 365 group and site owners first. If the person should see the whole site, I add them there. I only break inheritance on a folder when the exception is real, then I name who has what and I document it. OneDrive is the same idea with a smaller blast radius: sharing link versus direct access, and I revoke the link when the work is done. If it is still wrong, I test as the user, not as myself.

**MFA and SSPR for remote workers**

I enrol MFA when the account is created, not after they are stuck in a hotel. Authenticator first, a backup method second. SSPR is how remote staff unlock themselves without waiting on me: they must have those methods registered, and I test a reset once before I call the policy done. If MFA fails, I check the method, the device time, and Conditional Access-style blocks before I blame the user.

**Prioritising urgent tickets on my own**

Who cannot work, then who is a security risk, then the CEO, then everyone else. A down mailbox beats a font complaint. I timebox, I write what I tried, and I escalate instead of hoarding. Recurring noise becomes a how-to so it leaves the queue.
