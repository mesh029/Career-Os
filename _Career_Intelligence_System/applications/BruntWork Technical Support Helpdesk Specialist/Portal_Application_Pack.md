## 4. Paste-ready assessment answers

Edit lightly so they sound like you. Keep the facts.

### Tell us about yourself / your last position

I'm an IT support specialist with three years in IT support. At PATH, an international health NGO in Kenya, I worked as ICT Associate, where I ran first and second line support for 120+ users across 7 offices: ticket triage in Freshdesk and Zoho Desk under SLAs, laptops, printers, accounts and MFA, and Cisco Meraki network devices managed from the cloud dashboard. Response time improved by about 25%. I now work as Health Informatics Officer, supporting web-based health systems at 50+ sites, where I sit between the data and technical teams and fix platform access, device and data issues. I write troubleshooting guides and have trained 80+ staff. On PATH's Early Childhood Development programme I was the remote first line for field staff until it closed in 2025: Kobo forms, tablets, logins and sync problems fixed by phone and chat, plus remote onboarding and the programme's data close-out.

### Why this role?

It matches the three kinds of ticket I already solve: devices that go offline, data that looks wrong, and users who can't log in. I like owning a queue, keeping clients updated and following up with other teams until the problem is fixed. A Global Customer Support role on a hardware and SaaS product is where I want to grow, and permanent remote work lets me give it my full attention.

### How have you managed and prioritized support tickets using platforms such as Zendesk or Asana?

At PATH I ran the support queue in Freshdesk and Zoho Desk and tracked tasks in Asana. I prioritise by impact and SLA. Anything stopping someone working, or hitting a whole site, goes first, then tickets closest to breach, with quick fixes cleared in between. Clear statuses show what's waiting on whom. This cut our response time by about 25%. Zendesk works the same way.

### What steps would you take to troubleshoot a sensor, camera, or device connectivity issue before escalating it?

I check the dashboard first. When did it last report, and is it one device or the whole site? Several at once points to power or network. I try a remote reboot, then walk the person on site through power, cables and Wi-Fi. I log each step, and if it's still down I escalate with that summary. It's my routine with Cisco Meraki devices, CCTV cameras and biometric scanners.

### How do you track unresolved tickets and coordinate with technical teams to meet SLA targets?

I keep open tickets in a view sorted by SLA time and review it every shift. When a fix sits with another team, I send them the ticket number, client impact and deadline, and follow up before it's due. I update the client even when there's no news yet. If there's still no answer, I escalate. Recurring issues get grouped and sent to the technical team with a troubleshooting guide.

### An upset client calls. How do you handle it?

I let them finish, then acknowledge the impact: "I understand your cameras have been down since this morning and that affects your reporting." I confirm the details, tell them exactly what I'll do now, and give a time for my next update. Then I keep that promise. Staying calm and specific usually turns the call around.

### How do you manage competing priorities in a queue?

Impact and SLA first: anything stopping a client from working, or touching many users, goes to the top. Then tickets closest to breach. Quick wins get cleared between bigger issues so the queue doesn't build. I timebox diagnosis, write what I tried, and escalate instead of sitting on a ticket.

### How do you spot and report recurring issues?

I tag tickets consistently and look back at them weekly. When the same fault shows up at more than one site or client, I group the tickets, describe the pattern with examples and send it to the technical or data team. Then I write or update the troubleshooting guide so the next agent fixes it faster. At PATH, recurring data-entry errors across facilities led me to build SQL checks that cut reporting time by about 40%.

### Explain a technical issue to a non-technical person

A programme lead thought a shared folder had been deleted. Her access had changed after a colleague left. I showed her who still had access, restored hers, and wrote a five-line guide. No jargon, just what happened, what I did and what to do next time.

### Availability and working across time zones

I'm in Kenya and can work Monday to Friday during Melbourne business hours. Melbourne is currently 8 hours ahead, so a 9 AM start is 1 AM my time. I have a quiet home office, stable internet with a backup connection, and I'm flexible when global support needs altered hours.
