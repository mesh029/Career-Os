1. **The JD is the union of your actual job.** Servers, virtualisation, Active Directory, cloud, LAN/WAN/VPN/Wi-Fi, firewalls, DNS and DHCP, backups and DR, second and third-level support, incidents under SLA with RCA, security controls and patching, ICT projects and migrations, SOPs and documentation, user training, mentoring juniors, vendor coordination, asset and licence inventory. There is almost nothing on that list you have not done at PATH.
2. **Certifications: "strongly preferred" and you have one they name.** CCNA is on their list, plus AZ-900 for the Azure and O365 line. You are not guessing at this gate.
3. **Every named technology is real for you.** Windows Server 2016–2022, Linux, AD, VMware and Hyper-V, Azure and Microsoft 365, FortiGate and Meraki, Veeam and rsync. The requirement block reads like your skills section.
4. **"System *and* network administration"** is the rare phrasing that rewards your hybrid background instead of punishing it. Most candidates are one or the other. You have run both because you were the whole ICT function for your sites.
5. **They want someone who mentors and documents.** That is your differentiator over a pure break-fix admin: SOPs, knowledge base, 80+ staff trained, 15+ sessions.

## The three things to keep honest

- **AWS is familiarity, not operations.** Their cloud line says "AWS/Azure/O365" — you satisfy it through Azure and O365. Lead with those. If AWS comes up, say you have worked with it and Docker at a familiarity level and your depth is Azure. Do not claim AWS production administration.
- **Server room, not a tier-3 data centre.** The JD talks about data centre facilities, UPS and environmental controls. You have managed a server room including hardware health, power and environment. Say "server room and comms room" and let them scale it up. Do not describe yourself as having run a data centre.
- **Title.** Canonical titles stay: Health Informatics / HMIS Officer, ICT Associate, ICT Intern. The CV makes systems and network administration the spine so the three years reads correctly without touching a title.

## Agency-specific tactics

Reeds Africa Consult is a **recruitment consultancy and the end client is not named.** That changes three things:

1. **The CV does all the work.** A recruiter who is not a sysadmin screens first, so the CV leads with the exact nouns from the requirement block — Windows Server, Linux, Active Directory, VMware, Hyper-V, Azure, O365, CCNA — rather than with narrative.
2. **Ask who the client is** on the recruiter call, along with industry, team size, and whether there is on-call. You cannot judge the offer without it.
3. **Do not name a salary first.** Ask for the client's budgeted band. If pushed, **KES 170,000–200,000 per month** for a full-scope system and network administrator role in Nairobi; single figure **180,000**.

## Positioning

**System and network administrator who has owned the whole stack.** Someone who has run the servers, the identity, the network and the security for a multi-site operation at ~98% uptime, holds CCNA and AZ-900, and documents and trains as a matter of habit rather than when asked.

## Before you send

Confirm **CCNA is the exam certification** rather than a NetAcad course completion. It is "strongly preferred" here rather than mandatory, so this application survives either way — but you want to know before a recruiter asks.
