## How this coexists with the "narrow to health informatics" strategy

These are not in conflict. We run a **primary brand + capability layers** model:

- **Primary brand (front door):** Health Informatics / EMR–DHIS2 specialist. This wins interviews fastest and is your scarcest, most credible edge. It stays the default headline, summary, and #1 resume.
- **Capability layers (depth behind the door):** Software/full-stack, infrastructure/servers, networking, DevOps/Docker, consulting, and research. These are surfaced through **Projects, Featured, GitHub, the portfolio, and the IT-Operations/Engineering resume variant** — and drawn on when a specific role calls for them.

Result: focus where it counts, without hiding the broader engineer. When a job is engineering/infra/consulting-flavored, we lead with the relevant layer instead of health informatics.

## Evidence we should STRENGTHEN before claiming loudly
Claim these only once artifacts exist (otherwise soften to "familiarity"):
- Software/system architecture *consulting*, startup MVP strategy → need a writeup or client/testimonial.
- ETL pipelines, reverse proxies, performance tuning, Dockerized production → need a GitHub repo/README.
- "Code reviews / architecture reviews with other developers" → need a repo, PRs, or named collaboration.

**Rule:** capability language must have a clickable proof (repo, doc, screenshot, cert) or a verifiable story. No proof → downgrade the verb ("explored/familiar with") rather than drop it.
