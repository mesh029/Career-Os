# Application Intelligence — Network Engineer, Co-operative Bank of Kenya

**Apply score: ~45%. Stretch, not a priority.** Deadline 30 Sep 2026, ref NE/IID/2026, email application.

This is the first role in a while where the honest answer is that the door is probably shut, and it is worth being precise about why rather than dressing it up.

## The blocker

> "A Bachelor's degree ... **with possession of Cisco Certified Network Professional (CCNP), is a MUST.** Cisco Certified Internetwork Expert (CCIE) is preferred."

They capitalised MUST. That is not a nice-to-have written by a lazy HR drafter — it is a screening instruction, and at a bank of Co-op's applicant volume it will be applied mechanically before a human reads your experience. **You hold CCNA. CCNP is a different, higher certification and you cannot claim it.** Claiming it would be checked against Cisco's certification verification, and a bank withdraws offers over that.

## Three more gaps that are real

Even if the cert were waived, this is a level above your current band:

1. **Service Provider networks.** They want "extensive knowledge of Service Provider network and demonstrated experience working with SP equipment and protocols." That is carrier-side — MPLS, BGP at scale, provider handoffs. You have consumed ISP links; you have not operated SP equipment.
2. **The vendor stack is not yours.** Cisco you have. **Checkpoint** firewalls and **Avaya** telephony you do not. A bank core network runs on exactly those.
3. **UNIX depth.** AIX, Solaris, RHEL and OpenShift. You have Ubuntu and CentOS. Banks genuinely still run AIX, and this is not a bluffable line.

Add "design and development of complex LAN/WAN" and formal project leadership, and the honest picture is a strong multi-site LAN/WAN **operator** applying for a **core network engineer** seat.

## But this role tells you something useful

Co-op Bank just told you the exact price of admission to bank-grade network engineering, and it is **CCNP**. That is worth treating as a roadmap rather than a rejection:

- **CCNP Enterprise** is two exams: **ENCOR (350-401)** plus one concentration, commonly **ENARSI (300-410)**. There is no course requirement and no CCNA prerequisite to sit it.
- You already hold CCNA and run the gear daily, which is the hard part. Bank and telco network roles in Nairobi consistently gate on CCNP, so it unlocks a whole role class and a materially higher band than the KES 150–180k range you are quoting now.
- If you are serious about the network track rather than the sysadmin track, this is the single highest-return certification on your list — more than MD-102, more than anything else outstanding.

## If you decide to send it anyway

The cost is one email, so it is not irrational — just go in clear-eyed:

- Send to **jobs@co-opbank.co.ke** with reference **NE/IID/2026** in the subject, by **30 Sep 2026**.
- Lead the letter with **CCNA held and CCNP in progress** only if you have actually booked or started ENCOR. Do not write "in progress" as a figure of speech.
- Lead the CV with the parts that are genuinely strong: multi-site LAN/WAN at ~98% uptime, firewall and security policy, DR and business continuity, network documentation and inventory, IP telephony, TCP/IP.

**Never claim CCNP, CCIE, Checkpoint, Avaya, AIX, Solaris, OpenShift or Service Provider experience on this application.**
